Classical Ciphers

Classic Caesar Cipher
key = 3

Julius Caesar's cipher shifts every letter exactly 3 positions forward — the foundation of substitution cryptography.

Algorithm
  1. Assign: A→0, B→1, … Z→25
  2. Convert plaintext to numbers
  3. Add 3, then take mod 26
  4. Convert back to letters

Classic Affine Cipher
E(x) = (ax+b) mod 26

Combines multiplication and addition — two keys: a (must be coprime with 26) and b (additive shift). Decryption uses the modular inverse of a.

RSA Cryptography

RSA (Rivest–Shamir–Adleman, 1977) is an asymmetric algorithm relying on the practical difficulty of factoring the product of two large primes. It underpins most of modern secure internet communication.

RSA Key Generation
n = p × q

Enter two distinct primes p and q to generate a public/private key pair. The modulus n = p × q and φ(n) = (p−1)(q−1).
